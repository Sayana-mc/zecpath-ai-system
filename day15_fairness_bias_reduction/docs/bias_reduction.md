\# ZecPath Day 15 – Fairness, Normalization \& Bias Reduction



\## 1. Objective



The objective of Day 15 is to improve fairness, reduce potential bias, and standardize candidate evaluation in the ZecPath AI-powered resume screening system.



The Day 15 fairness pipeline processes the candidate ranking records generated by Day 14 and applies normalization, keyword-dependence reduction, score normalization, personal-attribute masking, and bias-indicator evaluation.



\## 2. Fairness Methods Implemented



The following fairness methods were implemented:



1\. Resume and candidate field normalization

2\. Keyword dependence reduction

3\. ATS score normalization

4\. Non-essential personal attribute masking

5\. Bias indicator calculation

6\. Fairness-adjusted candidate scoring

7\. Fairness validation through automated tests



\## 3. Candidate Field Normalization



Candidate records are normalized into a consistent structure before fairness evaluation.



The normalization process handles:



\- Candidate names

\- Job IDs

\- Job titles

\- ATS scores

\- Classification values

\- Score breakdowns

\- Source file information

\- Ranking information



Text values are normalized to reduce unnecessary formatting differences.



\## 4. Score Normalization



Candidate scores are standardized to a common 0–100 range.



The configured score range is:



\- Minimum score: 0

\- Maximum score: 100



Scores below the minimum are converted to the minimum value and scores above the maximum are converted to the maximum value.



This prevents invalid or inconsistent score values from affecting candidate comparison.



\## 5. Keyword Dependence Reduction



The Day 15 system reduces excessive dependence on keyword matching.



The configured keyword dependence limit is:



0.35



The fairness configuration also gives greater importance to semantic evaluation.



Configured semantic weight:



0.65



The objective is to reduce the possibility that candidates are ranked highly only because their resumes contain exact job-description keywords.



\## 6. Personal Attribute Masking



Non-essential personal attributes are masked before fairness evaluation.



The following attributes are masked:



\- candidate\_name

\- name

\- email

\- phone

\- mobile

\- address

\- date\_of\_birth

\- dob

\- gender

\- age

\- photo

\- nationality

\- religion

\- marital\_status



The masked candidate name is represented as:



\[MASKED]



These attributes are not used for candidate scoring.



\## 7. Protected Attributes



The Day 15 bias report confirms:



"protected\_attributes\_used\_for\_scoring": false



Therefore, protected/personal attributes are not used as scoring factors in the fairness evaluation.



\## 8. Fairness Score



The system stores both the original score and the fairness-checked score.



Important fields include:



\- fairness\_pre\_adjusted\_score

\- fairness\_score

\- fairness\_status

\- fairness\_rank



Example:



fairness\_pre\_adjusted\_score: 13.24

fairness\_score: 13.24

fairness\_status: FAIRNESS\_CHECKED

fairness\_rank: 8



The fairness score allows the system to maintain an auditable record of the candidate evaluation.



\## 9. Bias Indicator Calculation



Bias indicators are calculated separately for every job.



The system calculates:



\- Candidate count

\- Average score

\- Minimum score

\- Maximum score

\- Score gap

\- Bias warning



The score gap is evaluated against the configured bias warning threshold.



Configured threshold:



0.20



If the calculated score gap exceeds the configured threshold, the system generates a bias warning.

This configuration allows fairness policies to be modified without changing the core processing logic.
